# Special Relativity and Flat Spacetime

- Newton's inverse-square law: $$\mathbf{F} = \frac{GMm}{r^2}\mathbf{e}_{(r)}$$
- Newton's second law: $$\mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{a}$$
- Poisson's equation, with gravitational potential $\Phi$ and mass density $\rho$: $$\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$$
- acceleration in this case: $$\mathbf{a} = \nabla \Phi$$
- quick note on where this comes from because I can't seem to recall it from my mechanics course:
    - Let $\mathbf{g}$ denote the gravitational acceleration field: $$\mathbf{g} = -\nabla \Phi$$
    - Then using the gravitational Gauss' law: $$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{g} = -4\pi G\rho$$
    - Then substitute $\mathbf{g}$ and you have it.
- So in GR, we essentially replace each of them by descriptions for the curvature of spacetime.
- GR presents that gravity is not a "force", but rather a property of spacetime.
