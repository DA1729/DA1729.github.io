# Research Anxiety

I have my times with people where I -- with a self perception of a wise old man -- make certain "quotes." But I try not to make those cringe.

One thing that I find myself saying frequently -- if you have managed to shape your current situation so that you can actually follow what you care about and love, then almost all the anxiety you will suffer through will be "subject" related. Now I know that such things are said for the sake of it and any wise man will never take quotes as truth -- that's too black and white.

But over this past year -- I have experienced quite a lot of different things as a young researcher and I just cannot help myself but keep digging into this particular saying of mine and keep reformulating and extending it. So perhaps I'll end up with a novel long quote. The ultimate quote of my life ahahaha.

Although I have been leaning towards research and you could say also working towards building a life around it for a very long time, I could argue that me spending time figuring out why tf carrying works in subtraction in first-grade -- boy, my teachers thought that I was retarded... anyway. I cannot really pin-point a reason why I find myself so naturally gravitating towards research and more importantly maths. One of the reasons -- it requires you to just sit down and spend time with it, and atp it's not even about me loving that -- it's just what my mind can live with.

Ok, I do spend a lot of time with the applied stuff, but so far I have been living at the interface between pure and applied mathematics and honestly I think this suits me more than just living in pure math. Maybe I am saying it because my present situation doesn't really allow me to spend all the time there. And this condition is a superposition of a lot of stuff -- like my current major, ongoing projects, future planning -- which itself contains a lot of stuff like capital, and all other practical stuff (cuz I ain't a rich kid). I know about all such constraints, which is why, I almost never really advise following your passion because I know what all really delicate and important things can stop you from doing so.

But, I also don't endorse people who whine that they could not do what they love because of all the reasons -- and please don't take in extreme cases like wars and stuff like that, those are on a very different axis than I am putting the stuff I am currently discussing. I feel like, and please note that I don't give rigid and concrete opinions, but I have to succumb to wording my multi-dimensional opinions on stuff, so take all the things that I say with a fair amount of "useful ambiguity." Yeah, so, I feel like -- anyone, at least the people I normally interact or deal with on a daily basis, are privileged enough to find a really nice middle ground between all the practical constraints they have and their passion.

But this past year, I have realized that me saying that you will face situations in which your anxiety will be your "passion"-based anxiety fails quite catastrophically. So like, my anxiety will mostly be about "why tf can I not prove this theorem?" Maybe a proof doesn't exist, you know -- Godel. But, I would like yall to think about the source of anxiety. Let's say that I figured out a way to get good food for free with a really nice place to stay -- a small village in the mountains perhaps, so that I don't become totally isolated, and all I need to care about is doing mathematics. Now, in that case, will I actually be anxious about the proof?

Now things get interesting, and I am thinking as I am typing -- I do not have these posts planned. So, one could argue that ok, given the conditions, why would I be working at all? To this, I am pretty sure, given me -- I would keep studying mathematics, and would be even more happy. And this is the catch, I will be happy despite the fact that the conjecture might not exist at all. I would just be happy that I am only thinking about math.

I could write more and more on this, and maybe I will more, but I think atp, I can go ahead with the main point I wanted to convey with the post, cuz we have enough context on us. So, the anxiety induced in this situation, might turn out to be even worse I would say compared to someone who just very early on, succumbed to something fully practical. Because, the "ops'" anchors for anxiety for researchers, are something that threaten the existence of the middle ground that they have worked incredibly hard implicitly along their way while explicitly working hard on the research subject. And this middle ground is often very sacred for the researchers, really inter-mixed with one's assumed purpose.

I'm doing good btw -- it was just one of those moments when I realized that when you get out of such a situation, and are all happy, you seem to really forget the intense anxious feelings and at least for me, there is this brief moment in such situations, where I realize that this was not the last time.
