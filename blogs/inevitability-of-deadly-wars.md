# Inevitability of Deadly Wars

I have been reading a lot about how various groups reacted to WW1/2 and the Cold War. I would really like to think that we are close to a robust social structure, with no need for war and everything solved diplomatically, but then I'd be just fooling myself.

But think about it: the idea of Game Theory, even to one of the intelligent academics back in the 1800s, would have sounded absurd, because you need observations from a developed society to be able to understand such concepts.

I don't know how right I am, formally speaking, but the only way to have a society with no possibility of war is to have a planet with a uniform distribution of natural resources and conditions, and for everyone to have literally the same consciousness, i.e., nobody knows or can reason more than anyone else.

An extract from my recent [blog](post.html?kind=blog&post=towards-a-theory-of-agents):

> Now, if you really think about it, or maybe think hard and try to break my following assertion, you should be able to see that I can describe an agent in the context of the entire system, rather than how it functions internally, using the following three 'quantities':
>
> - Information
> - Action
> - Decision.

I have discussed this particular thing with multiple people and read many similar arguments, and everyone seems to agree with it. But that's essentially because, collectively speaking, we are not aware enough to realize the existence of any other such dimension controlling our society.

I could go ahead and hypothesize that society models that take into account only a limited number of dimensions often lead to unstable systems, with the resulting instability manifesting as large-scale wars.
