# What Can't Be Offloaded

I want to ask one question and stay on it for the whole of this: when we offload our thinking to machines, what exactly are we handing over, and what, if anything, can we never hand over?

Before I start, let me get one thing out of the way, because otherwise this gets dragged into a fight I am not interested in having. I am not going to argue about whether AI can really think, or how far it will get. Assume it gets as capable as you like. Assume it does every last thing the loudest people in the room say it will. I am not asking you to believe that, or to want it to be true. Just grant it, for the length of this piece, and look with me at the question sitting right next to that one, the one almost nobody is actually asking.

## The offloading

Here is a hypothesis I ran into a while back, and it has not left me since. Over the course of evolution our digestive system first grew and then shrank, and one theory is that we began offloading part of the digestive work to things outside the body, using fire to cook and pre-break-down our food. For most of our history the brain grew too, and there is some evidence it may now be shrinking. My guess is that the same move is happening one floor up: we have started outsourcing cognition to external systems, silicon, and now AI.

I have this whole picture of humans forever finding new ways to condense and compress the knowledge they gather. We invented writing, then books, then computers and high-density storage. Each one holds more of the load so the mind has to hold less.

And here is the part that matters. All that accumulated rigor, the proofs and the formal machinery, especially in something like mathematical physics, was quietly turning into friction against free and intuitive thinking. It was making the fields less and less accessible, in the sense that you had to swallow an enormous volume of existing "knowledge" before you had enough instinct to see anything new. I actually feel mathematics has become more accessible to me over the last couple of years, and I have thought about it enough to not hand all the credit to my own maturing. Something else got lighter too.

So offloading is not new, and it is not the enemy. It is the oldest move we have.

## The invariant

A while back I gave the thing underneath all this its own name, because I kept needing to point at it and had no word. Call it the dvole: the irreducible bundle of intelligence, consciousness, intuition, instinct, appreciation of beauty, and existential wonder at nature. Really it is one drive, to observe nature, build intuition about it, model it, and feel haunted by how beautiful it is.

Through every one of those offloading steps, the container changes. Eight billion biological brains. Then fewer, less cluttered brains sitting on top of a massive silicon layer. Then who knows, integrated substrates, distributed things we do not have words for yet. The label "human" might one day be historical sentiment. Through all of it, the dvole is what continues. Containers change; the dvole is the invariant.

That is the thing I want to protect here, and to protect it I have to be exact about what is being offloaded and what is not. So here is the center of the whole piece.

## A box with a person in it

From here I am going to lean on one assumption, and I want to name it out loud so you can throw everything after it away if you do not accept it. The assumption is Penrose's: that genuine understanding is non-algorithmic, that there is something in real understanding that no algorithm, however deep, reproduces. I am not proving this. I am assuming it. If it is false, everything below is false with it, and that is fine.

Now the thought experiment. Put a person in a box. Give them the full transformer algorithm, all the weights, all the arithmetic, and unlimited paper and time. A prompt comes in. The person, by hand, does every multiplication and every addition the algorithm specifies, and eventually writes out the response. In principle, that response is the same one you would get from the same algorithm running on a GPU.

I should be honest that this is an idealization. Nobody is running a trillion parameters by hand. It is an "in principle" argument, the same kind as a frictionless plane or Maxwell's demon, and it only works if you grant the in-principle part. Grant it.

Now look at what we have. Two systems, a GPU and a human with a pencil, coupled to the universe through completely different physical processes. The GPU runs on voltages and currents. The person runs on neurons, or honestly on whatever a person is made of. Two utterly different physical couplings, and the same output falls out of both.

So the output does not care which substrate produced it. It is invariant under the change of substrate.

A while back I was speculating about qualia and I ended up here: an intelligent system never receives the world directly, it receives it through some channel, and different systems have different channels, $K_A \neq K_B$. Different couplings to reality produce fundamentally different epistemic systems, not better or worse, just different. If that is what qualia is, the specific physical coupling a system has to reality, then anything that survives a change of that coupling cannot be carrying it. The output survives the change of coupling. So the output carries no qualia.

That is the whole hinge. The output, the actual intelligent-looking product, is one layer: pure algorithm, portable, substrate-free, and by the argument above, qualia-free. It is exactly the kind of thing that has always been offloadable. The dvole is the other layer: the coupling itself, the part tied to a real physical substrate, the part that by assumption is not an algorithm at all. AI lives entirely in the first layer. The dvole is what is left when you subtract that layer off.

Let me be careful, because this looks like Searle's Chinese room and it is not quite the same claim, and the difference is the whole reason it holds. Searle uses one room and has to insist there is no understanding inside it. I do not need that, and I am not claiming it. The human in my box obviously has qualia. My point is narrower and therefore safer: whatever qualia the human has does not enter the output, because a system with a completely different physical coupling produces the identical output. The qualia is real. It just does not show up in what gets handed out.

## The same thing, from the other side

There is a second way people try to hand real intelligence to the machine, and it is worth closing off too, because it fails for the same reason.

Suppose, just suppose, that the purpose of a qualia is to uncover and model the whole universe. I do not actually believe the universe is sitting there waiting to be finished, and I will come back to that, but grant it for a second. Say one qualia can do this and another cannot, then fine, call the first one superior. Now say silicon is the superior qualia. If the way it gets there is algorithmic, then by the box, a human can run that same algorithm by hand and get there too. Slower, of course, maybe absurdly slower, but slower is a property of how fast you grind the algorithm, not a property of the qualia. So both arrive. And the moment both arrive, the ranking has nothing left to rank. The supposed superiority was never a superiority of qualia. It was just possession of an algorithm, and algorithms do not belong to a substrate.

And the reason I only granted that finish line for a second: by my own definition, the dvole is open-ended. A complete model of everything would end the very wondering that makes it a dvole in the first place. So there is no finish line to win. I granted one only to show that even then, on the terms most generous to the machine, the machine still does not come out ahead.

## What people are actually afraid of

If you follow the two arguments, most of the dread around AI turns out to be a case of mistaken identity. People are not really afraid that the machine can think. They are afraid because somewhere they have quietly identified themselves with the part that is being offloaded, the grinding, the mechanical layer. And that part was never the thing worth being.

I have said this before more bluntly, so I will just say it again: AI is taking your jobs because your beautiful mind is not supposed to be doing that. The work a machine can lift off you was the clutter. You are the coupling, not the clutter. If that lands, every worry about AI reorganizes itself, and most of them get much smaller.

## Not black, not white

I want to be clear that I am not doing prophecy here. I have no idea how far this goes, how much gets poured into it, how much it reshapes things. I am doing something narrower, which is refusing the black-and-white version.

Take the most optimistic story, the one where silicon fully internalizes embodied experience, qualia, the felt difference between a cold morning and a warm one. Even granting all of that, the "alien superintelligence leaving biology behind" ending does not follow. Because to perfect that system, to make it energy efficient enough to sustain itself, richly sensory, socially adaptive, you are no longer building something that transcends life. You are rediscovering the thing evolution already spent billions of years optimizing. Perfecting AGI, on its own terms, converges on perfecting an organism. And if instead you strip all of that out, the qualia, the embodiment, the biological anchoring, you do get something genuinely alien, but you have also removed the only foundation on which it could understand or care about anything human at all. It will just count atoms in the universe on a loop forever. Your table could be doing that right now.

Either way the clean discontinuity collapses. Life does not operate in extremes, and neither, it turns out, does the logic of building something meant to surpass it.

---

So if I take Penrose seriously, and I have said all along that this rests on him, the dvole can only ride a substrate whose physics actually takes part in the thinking. A plain algorithm on a plain computer, however fast, however much it dazzles in that first layer, is never that. It is the newest and best container we have ever built for the clutter, which is exactly what we should want it to be, and exactly why it is not a threat to the only thing I have been trying to point at this whole time.

Maybe we integrate with silicon, maybe we offload almost everything onto it. As long as someone, somewhere, on whatever substrate, is still awed by all the different ways there are to perceive a single number, nothing has been lost. And if it ever turns out that the answer to the universe is just raw computation with no deep interpretation underneath, then honestly I am simply not interested in studying it. I do not think it is. The probabilistic core of quantum mechanics is, to me, enough to settle that the universe is not merely raw computation with the noise cleaned out. A probability is a probability. No amount of computation collapses it ahead of time. The best you can ever do is study, measure, and then stand there and appreciate what you saw.

That is as far as I am willing to walk it today. Take the core of it, not the words, the words carry maybe ten percent of what I actually mean, and go make your own conclusions.
