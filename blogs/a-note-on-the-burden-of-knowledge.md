It's a fairly easy observation. All it says is that it becomes increasingly difficult to "contribute" to any field as the amount of prior work in that field increases. ## Burden of Knowledge as a Potential Ceiling

When I first thought seriously about BoK, the first thing that occurred to me was whether it could serve as a ceiling on society's collective knowledge. Clearly, that's not the case, as can be worked out with a little logical thinking. But it gives us (at least me) a reason to think about it formally and investigate which particular things are affected by this burden.

Before moving any further, we must make one fact clear. A ceiling for an individual and a ceiling for society are different things. This realisation alone requires careful outlining, but I am forcing myself not to get right on to it.

## BoK due to Integration of Fields

This will be the last short section of this short post. Another major, and perhaps one of the fundamental, origins of this burden is the fact that the generated knowledge is classified into discrete subjects.

This requires us to study more from the other fields, and then try to establish connections that nobody has yet explicitly worked out. With this perspective, accumulated knowledge transforms from something to be retrieved to a pool for further inference.
