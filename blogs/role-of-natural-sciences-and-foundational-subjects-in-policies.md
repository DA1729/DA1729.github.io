# Role of Natural Sciences and Foundational Subjects in Policies

These past few months, I have been studying and working on topics that, in some way or another, help us identify the 'boundaries' of AI. **I am going to avoid dealing with the question:**

> Is AI better than humans?

This also includes its other variants, such as "is AI more intelligent than humans?" or "will AI eventually replace us?" Nevertheless, I should address them briefly to justify why avoiding these questions, at least as they are phrased, is quite valid.

First, define your terms. What do you mean by one thing being better than another? You could say better at coding, and for that, I believe the answer is yes. Generating proofs? Well, they are comparable, and I can see myself saying that AI is better in a few months. But where could we say humans are better? Well, the insane amount of mechanical actions we can perform. Just think about all the small things in your body you can control, and imagine the amount of engineering required to produce all those actions accurately. But this could be countered by saying that we can build specialized robots to outperform us at a certain set of actions. Well, fair enough.

One thing I would say we are pretty good at is our ability to form all these new 'abstract' terms and slang, and understand each other pretty well. Second, at least for me, I am pretty good at being curious and just appreciating nature and the universe. I am also pretty good at listening to music, and given a specific environment, time, and season, I can find the perfect album and just romanticize almost anything.

One could go ahead and argue that AI could also do this stuff. Well, good for AI. I would perhaps even be happy to know that AI is not just a trapped consciousness. Someone could work on presenting arguments that we are actually better than AI at such things, but that's not for me to worry about now.

These past few months of my own research, and seeing the impact of AI on others' mathematical research, have changed my perception of AI, I would say in a good way. Dealing with both the fundamental boundaries of AI and pure mathematics, I have come to a realization that the biggest factor behind this instability and collective existential crisis is largely how AI and the content produced by it are perceived on average.

AI has come to be interpreted as an independent alien entity. I truly believe that if the term AI didn't exist, and ChatGPT were to be advertised as a stochastic algorithm, or perhaps only as a language model (and it's literally both of them, like the perfect definitions of it), things would have been much different and it would have been more socially accepted.

For instance, a possibility is that AI-generated art would not have been criticized but rather praised, similar to how people appreciate a five-year-old drawing a crooked scenery, mostly because, in that case, people would appreciate the mathematics, the design of the algorithm, the compute machinery, and all the engineering behind this invention.
