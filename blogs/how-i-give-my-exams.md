# How I give my exams

Any reasonable person will not get ragebaited with what I am writing today, but if you are an "unreasonable" person, here is a short preface -- I am aware that special cases exist. Still, I cannot write about all of them, and everything I assert and conclude applies to an average/expected scenario.

I don't really like people who say stuff like -- "I don't get why people do this and that." OK, they do something that's retarded from your perspective, and you are aware of that, then I believe just telling yourself they are just retarded should be enough. And at the end, we all know it's for showing off your "superiority" or to initiate a rant or discussion on it with someone who also thinks a particular thing is retarded.

I won't say that I don't do this honestly. I have done this a lot, and even now, though not as often, I still do it for the same reason I stated above: to spark a conversation/rant with someone who also thinks that certain people and things are just retarded. But I have just started asserting those things now, rather than acting all naïve and oblivious. So here is an assertion: for most of the college exams (especially Indian colleges, cuz the admin and studies are retarded here), you do not have to keep writing for the entirety of the exam time and ask doubts regarding the paper, well, only if you are retarded, and in that case you will find yourself guilty for all this.

Now, I am not going to speak for them; instead, I am just going to pen down my exam giving and preparation philosophy. I really like to sit down with a subject, spend time with it, especially in maths and physics, which are exactly the subjects I am studying during my bachelor's.

I have not really enjoyed my physics courses. Because I don't like physics? NO, I love physics; it's just that my college's physics department is very experimental and observational rather than theoretical, and in the end, even theoretical professors focus more on engineering developments than on theories purely for the sake of physics and meta. Which is absolutely fine for them, and I don't hate people who do experimental and observational stuff. It's only that the way these fields are approached is highly incompatible with my interests and personality.

I believe it is better demonstrated with my previous post -- ["Rant on Physicists."](https://da1729.github.io/post.html?post=rant-on-physicists&kind=blog) I had just learned the Hilbert Space formulation of Quantum Mechanics, and it felt very enlightening compared to how it was taught to me during my college course. Might have done justice to the field from an engineering perspective, but really made a joke out of it from a theoretical and mathematical perspective. Same for all the subsequent courses I took—thermodynamics and statistical mechanics, optics, etc.

Now, these being so incompatible with me, I always approached studying for these courses in a very mechanical, rushed manner, studying a day before the exams and then just managing to score decent, if not good, in the past semesters. Story for every physics paper I have given -- I have the intuition and the structure behind solving it, owing to my long hours spent on mathematics, but for the question papers I have faced, it can barely take you any further. But, it's enough for me to judge that the problems are NOT DIFFICULT AT ALL -- literally no brainer. Now, you can be an experimental physicist and have my respect, but how tf do you expect me to do that when I can literally infer everything about you as a researcher from the question papers you set?

OK, so I have seen my batchmates prepare for the exams, study these subjects, and attend all the lectures right from the start of the semester. I have enough grounds to assume that you actually know the formulae I can only envision during exams. Then how the fuck are all of you sitting for the entire THREE HOURS of the exam time?

I can NEVER sit for most of the exams I take here in college for more than 40 minutes, whether it's a midterm or an end-of-term exam. For both types of courses -- ones I enjoy (only the maths courses I have taken lol) or the ones that irritate the hell out of me (almost all the physics courses). Now, I am not speaking for maths courses, even though I remember scoring full marks in Number Theory (both in midterm and end-of-term) and leaving the exam hall within 25 minutes. But, for maths courses, you can always think a bit more and get your solution. But again, given the type of exams here in Indian colleges, you are assumed to have memorised some stuff -- obviously, some professors are exceptions to this. Still, again, I am speaking on average.

I also do this other thing: no matter how prepared I am for the exam, I always sit on the first bench. Now, note that I am the person who does not attend any lectures. So it's always fun to see the professor see me for the first time and confidently take the first seat. Now, the reason behind this is that I do not cheat or copy others to score more than I can in that exam. And I am not going to give moral reasons behind this. In fact, given how morally flawed and IDK the right "worst" adjective here, the admin and many professors, the moral ground justifying not cheating in exams has vanished.

It takes TOO MUCH effort. Like, I already don't want to be in the exam hall, and it took too much effort to get myself there; now you expect me to push my peripheral vision and discretion? HELL NO. I have no business putting effort into something to justify my expertise in such an artificial way. I cannot care less about these exams. I have so many other things to study.

Now, the funny thing is, if I were to be in college like 60 to 70 years back, I think I would be scoring well in my examinations, simply because I would not have had access to the internet, which allows me to study all the things I want and in a way that really suits me. I would have been mostly invested in my major, with the library as the primary source of knowledge, and, in the end, I like studying; I have managed to score exceptionally well with very little effort in my mathematics courses.

A few months back, I was deciding whether to regret not taking my physics courses seriously, because I don't know when I'll next find myself in a position to study physics actively. But now, honestly, I am glad that I did not take them seriously. They would make my thinking and approach dull. I realised this while reading Neumann's book on Quantum Mechanics and studying the Hilbert Space formulation. If I had taken the courses seriously, my prior in physics would have gotten messed up so badly that it would have really affected my posterior.

I have my midterm examinations going on, and again, I am dealing with most of the courses the same way. Still, this semester, apart from my 2 mathematics courses, there's a physics course I can take seriously, since the professor teaching it gets it. I am not going to create unnecessary mystery around this subject; it's General Relativity. Due to my research internship at KU Leuven, I arrived on campus 10 days before my exams with 8 subjects I had not studied at all.

Getting back on campus was a bittersweet experience. Sweet, because a few of my good friends are here, and bitter, or I should just say straight up disgusting -- retarded college-mates, admin and instructors. I ignore most of them because, OMG, they think so shallowly. I have become immune to most of the things that ragebait people, but I have not become immune to having discussions with them. Not that everyone's that way, some of them are fine and good, I don't talk to them simply because our wavelengths don't match, which is fine. But people are having discussions about me taking General Relativity, even though I am not pursuing physics research. And I am just going to stop here, and it should be enough to describe their shallow reasonings.

Also, as I arrived on campus pretty late, I was not able to study GR as well as I wanted to, but it's fine, I have half a semester left, and I don't think I am leaving GR anytime soon, even after this semester.

Another note: I am performing really well in the courses I have prepared in a shallow manner now, with only a few hours of preparation. I don't know what's causing that; I will probably figure it out eventually, but I don't mind.
