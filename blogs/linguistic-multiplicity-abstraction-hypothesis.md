## Core hypothesis

- A person knows, say, two languages $\Rightarrow$ two symbolic encodings of abstract and conceptual structures (somewhat overlapping with each other).

- Now with this, repeated mapping of one concept to multiple linguistic symbols may result in strengthening of the following separation: $$\text{concept}\neq \text{word}.$$

- Say our abstract structure is $X$. A monolingual may be represented as follows: $$X \leftrightarrow A_X.$$

- Whereas, our bilingual fellow may be represented as: $$A_X \leftarrow X \rightarrow B_X.$$

- Therefore, the underlying $X$ may become more "psychologically" salient as an independent representation.

## Some things in an attempt to make my representation less symbolic

I had a little more cold-brew left when I finished thinking and writing till here, so I thought, why not try to work on the stuff I might need in order to make my framing of the hypothesis above less symbolic in nature.

### Language balance

Let $$p_A + p_B = 1$$where $p_A, p_B$ = proportions of meaningful use/exposure. Now I think it makes sense, because of information theory, and how the entropy of a distribution is defined, to work with the following term: $$B = -\frac{p_A\log p_A + p_B \log p_B}{\log 2}.$$ Logs are in base 2.

Now why this? Note exactly when $B$ attains its highest value. Exactly when $p_A = p_B$, in other words, when they are perfectly balanced.

So, now I can say -- higher the value of $B$, higher the bilingual balance. Also, this quantity can easily be extended to more languages.

To separate myself completely from my initial framing, I can define another quantity $M$, where $$M_A = \text{mastery of }A$$ $$M_B = \text{mastery of }B$$and then just say that $$B \neq M_A \neq M_B.$$Easy.

This allows me to say -- a person can be highly balanced but weak in both languages, highly balanced and strong in both, or unbalanced while highly proficient. I can envision them -- balance and mastery -- being two orthogonal dimensions in a linear space, and it would also make sense given the empirical studies of psychology.

### Verbal dependence

Define: $$V = \text{degree to which cognition relies on verbal/linguistic representation}.$$

And now, the hypothesis in its most compact representation: $$B\uparrow \Rightarrow V\downarrow$$

Not because the bilingual person lacks words, but because no single linguistic system "monopolizes" the conceptual/abstract structure.

### I am probably going to drop this term in the future

Now, I am just overfitting things only for the sake of writing, and I am not really satisfied with this term, but still, it would not hurt to document it. Also, it has happened to me before that a term I thought was just an overfitting anchor turned out to be the fundamental base and nullified other terms I thought were more fundamental.

Define: $$R = \text{capacity or tendency to manipulate relations, concepts and structures independently of their verbal labels}$$

The relation that I am proposing now is: $$V\downarrow \Rightarrow R\uparrow$$Therefore:$$B \rightarrow V \rightarrow R$$or approximately: $$B\uparrow \Rightarrow V\downarrow \Rightarrow R\uparrow$$

Now that I am writing it again, I think that this term will definitely survive. Anyway...
