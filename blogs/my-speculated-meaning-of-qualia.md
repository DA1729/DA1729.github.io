# My speculated meaning of qualia

Last Friday, I was out with my friends, we were chillin' at this really nice pub in Leuven -- [Fiere Margriet](https://www.instagram.com/fieremargrietleuven/) (I am in no way sponsored by them lol, but it's a really nice pub), and I got this really interesting thought, and did some scribbling in my research diary (this thought and piece is NOT related to my research, that diary was all I had at the moment). Us humans and also the silicon people (this is going to be important for what I am writing further, and to all the silicon people, feel free to call us humans carbon people ahahah), have our internal processes -- blood flow, quantum fields switching the transistors, etc.

UNCOUNTABLE such processes, we might be aware of many such effects and have a lot of theories, but we are centuries away from having a relativity and quantum aware model of the respiratory system, CPU, neurosystem, etc. Even centuries feel very short in this case, given the amount of complexity and everything.

We have models (useful models I'd say, as biological modelling has really transformed the search for cancer's cure), but they safely ignore such effects, as it makes sense. The relativistic correction for blood flow would be in the order of $10^{-18}$. And the point I am trying to make is not that these ignored-effects in our coarse models accumulate and could result in a big offset from reality.
What I am trying to say is, for our existence, for anything's existence, we don't have to worry at all about modelling them. They are just there. Blood particles do experience the gravity between them, they do experience electrostatic force from electrons of other blood particles, and uncountable such effects for uncountable particles, let alone blood particles. Same for anything: a computer, a tree, or your table.

With this, I got to thinking about how we have divided our study of the universe into distinct subjects -- mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, neuroscience, etc. These are useful for operational purposes, like very useful and necessary, but the underlying world is not really partitioned into those disciplines. It just works, everything, all at once (ok don't give me a relativity lecture, and the problem with everything happening at once).

The thing to really observe is that different systems, as my speculation says, experience (when viewed as a whole) different sorts of these delicate, underlying processes. Gravity between two electrons in a metal and between two blood particles should give us a nice distinction (although I can imagine the horrors of actually trying to measure them).

Now, as usual, I get a lot of speculative thoughts, but I feel like I am really going to think about it for a long time. Yeah, so I speculate that this, all these small processes happening, and I won't say that we can enumerate them or that they are separable, as it will literally defeat the leap I am trying to take from the usual view of formal physics to this really abstract, ambiguous yet formal enough view of physics and reality. I speculate that this exact thing is what [C. I. Lewis](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/lewis-ci/) coined -- *[qualia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/qualia/).* And the reason this clicks for me is [Roger Penrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Penrose): he argued that human understanding is non-algorithmic, that it is exactly what separates us from any algorithmic intelligence, no matter how advanced, one can come up with, and he tied that to human consciousness specifically. But what I am thinking, with my reasoning and speculation, is that everything has its qualia.

Now, I must use a little formalism (idc if it's right or not) else I will have to write a lot of things to explain, I would say really compact ideas.

Let the world have state $X_t$, with dynamics $$X_{t+1} = \Phi(X_t, \zeta_t).$$

An intelligent system $S$ does not directly receive $X_t$. It receives observations through some channel: $$Y_t^s \sim K_S(\cdot \mid X_{\leq t}).$$

Different intelligent systems can have different channels: $$K_A \neq K_B.$$

Now this gives me a reasonable "principle":

> Different couplings to reality can produce fundamentally different epistemic systems.

Not necessarily better or worse. But different.

A human, a text-only model (your ChatGPT), a robot with UV sensors, a distributed sensor network, and some alien organism could all be receiving radically different projections of the same universe.

Another thing I think I can settle upon -- **having lots of separate information does not necessarily determine the joint structure.**

Going to end this part right here, because I need to think more, in order to present more sound speculations. I believe I will be writing on it for years, not in continuation.
